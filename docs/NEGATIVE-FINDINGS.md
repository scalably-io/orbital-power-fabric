# Negative findings — kept, never deleted

Each entry: date · finding · evidence class · experiment/claim ID · what it kills or constrains.

## 2026-09-27
1. **Handoff mirror model omits mirror tilt.** A mirror sending sunlight to a ground target has its normal bisecting the Sun and target directions; in a dawn-dusk orbit (Sun ≈ 90° from the target) it intercepts A·cos 45° = 0.707·A. Mirror needed for one GB300 rack at full capture: 520 → 736 m². DERIVED (geometry). E002, `tests/test_physics.py::test_mirror_tilt_dawn_dusk`.
2. **Handoff footprint is the overhead-only best case.** At 625 km the solar image covers 26.3 km² at 90° elevation, 68 km² at 45°, 167 km² at 30°, 408 km² at 20° (slant range + oblique ground incidence). DERIVED. E002.
3. **A per-rack mirror is physically useless for PV.** The handoff's 520 m² mirror spreads its light over ≥26 km², adding 0.0207 W/m² — 2×10⁻⁵ of full sun. PV and inverters do not operate usefully there (sourced low-light curve pending, R5). Useful irradiance needs km²-class mirror area per footprint (10 W/m² → 0.25 km²; 100 W/m² → 2.5 km², overhead case). Branch C is a GW-scale-or-nothing architecture, not a per-rack one. DERIVED. E004.
4. **LEO power beaming is visibility-starved.** One ground site sees one 625 km satellite (30° mask) ~0.5% of the time. Even as a lower bound (PV mass only, zero transmitter/structure mass), B needs on the order of 1,000 globally distributed receivers to beat orbital compute on lifecycle launched mass per kW. DERIVED from handoff-seeded values; re-run with sourced values. E004.
5. **The handoff never compares against Earth-only power.** Every orbital-energy option must also beat grid / ground PV + storage (branch T). Added as a mandatory comparator (PREREGISTRATION).
6. **NASA's own SBSP study assumes hardware ~11× lighter than anything flown.** Its baseline implies ~910–940 W/kg whole-system at PV output (R2-018, DERIVED); flown iROSA wings are 84.6 W/kg (R2-093), flown missions cluster near 30 W/kg, Caltech's 2025 "today" estimate is 25 W/kg (R2-089, R2-067). The handoff's "100–140 W/kg class" is corrected to 71–98 W/kg for large flown wings.
7. **NASA concludes SBSP is not cost-competitive "even if access to space were free", all else constant** (R2-028; baseline $0.61 and $1.59/kWh, launch 71–77% of cost, R2-023).
8. **No net power has ever been delivered from space to the ground.** Caltech MAPLE was detection, not power transfer, by Caltech's own definition; best measured in-orbit figures are MAPLE 3.4–3.7% DC-to-DC over 30 cm and PRAM-FX ~8% sunlight-to-RF (R2-053, R2-044). Caltech's 2025 system estimate: 2.77% sunlight-to-grid today, 24-h average at GEO (R2-065) vs the handoff chain's 12.97%.
9. **Every SBSP beam design exceeds the public exposure limit at beam centre by 8–125×** (vs 10 W/m²; R2 dossier) — receivers need exclusion zones, which feeds receiver acreage and siting.
10. **Optical-core energy figures understate system energy by ~318×.** The only measured full photonic AI processor (Lightmatter, Nature 2025) runs 0.81 TOPS/W at system level against 262 TOPS/W for the photonic tensor core alone — parity with GPUs, not an advantage (R3-043…045).
11. **The handoff's photonic energy anchors are not what they claim.** "~370 fJ/op full-system prototype" (C016) is a component-model estimate, not a measurement; "56.8 / 9.6 / 1.9–3 fJ/MAC" (C015) is heater power divided by a hypothetical 88-wavelength throughput for a fibre-link device that is not an AI accelerator; "65 TOPS" (C017) multiplies one channel by 124 and its 1.5 pJ/op is an estimate, 94% laser (R3 dossier).
12. **Photonics does not fix LLM decode.** Measured GH200 costs: weight fetch is ~99% of (weight + MAC) energy at batch 1, ~74% at batch 64 (R3-088). A complete photonic transformer built from best published parts would be ~2.8–3.1 pJ/MAC in decode (DERIVED, never built as one system) — the HBM weight stream dominates. Orbital photonic heat is ~2.8–9.3 kW per PMAC/s of decode, not the handoff's 1.9–57 W (R3-106…108). Prefill at 5-bit is the only regime with a 1.4–2.8× edge over B300 FP8.
13. **Thermo-optic weights put a ~2 pJ/MAC floor on photonic MVM at 10 GS/s, independent of mesh size.** Holding a Haar-random matrix in a Clements mesh costs 0.33 W (4×4) / 1.23 W (8×8) at the vendor's <20 mW/π (R4-033); per MAC that is ≈1.9–2.5 pJ at 10 GS/s and ≈19–25 pJ at 1 GS/s, because heater count and MACs per cycle both scale as N² (E012, DERIVED). That alone rivals a GPU system's ~6.8 pJ/MAC (R3). Photon-native weight holding (non-volatile / self-holding phase) is therefore a first-order requirement, not an optimisation — the test chip measures this term directly.
14. **A rack-sized orbital mirror delivers no AC power on Earth** *(wording corrected E028: it is below every utility inverter's start threshold, not zero irradiance; the threshold is 2.6 W/m² with an ideal module, ~5 W/m² at 50% low-light efficiency, R5-069)*. Utility inverters (≥1 MW, n = 169, NREL SAM CEC list) start at a median 0.348% of rating → ≈2.6 W/m² irradiance at the ATB loading ratio 1.32 (R5-068/069, independently recomputed E016). The handoff mirror's 0.0207 W/m² is ~130× below start-up.
15. **Firm solar on Earth is 4–15× cheaper than NASA's own space-solar baseline:** $104–167/MWh for 97–99.4% of hours (Ember, R5-027/028) vs $610–1,590/MWh (R2-023). The real terrestrial constraint is time, not cost: median 61 months from interconnection request to operation (LBNL 2026, R5-042).
16. **Solar-pumped lasers lose to PV + fibre laser today:** 4.64% measured sunlight→laser vs ≈12% for PV → DC-DC → fibre laser (R6-123); the often-quoted 51.7 W/m² "record" is a simulation (R6-065). No measured system routes a band of sunlight into photonic compute.
17. **Correction to my own claim (E015):** direct photon→charge read-out is the biggest optical lever only without time integration. With T = 32 the laser term falls to ~2 fJ/MAC and what remains is activation memory (~38 fJ) and weight fetch.
18. **Sunlight cannot replace the laser in a single-mode photonic computer (H3 killed for throughput).** By the brightness theorem (D18) one spatial mode collects at most 64 nW of sunlight per THz of band at 1,550 nm; even with a 1 fF photon→charge node that supports ≤ 8×10⁵–4×10⁶ five-bit reads per second per output (1–5 THz band, 20 dB loss) — 2.5×10³–1.25×10⁴× below a 10 GS/s engine (E018; figures corrected E028 to the script's output). Sunlight can only feed slow or massively multimode (incoherent) optics; the laser is also a minor term (~5%) in the energy stack, so removing it saves little.
19. **The "long-lived energy layer" premise fails in LEO: satellites live ~5 years, like GPUs.** SpaceX's and Starcloud's FCC filings list a 5-year satellite life (R1-023, R1-079); SpaceX's 424B4 books satellites at 3–5 years vs servers 5–6 years and states satellite life is "inherently shorter than" the IT it hosts (R1-034/035). The handoff's lifecycle argument (refresh GPUs on the ground, keep long-lived energy hardware in orbit) needs energy satellites to outlive GPUs several-fold — true for GEO-class lifetimes, not for LEO at 500–650 km. It also removes most of branch A's refresh penalty, since A's compute is relaunched with the satellite anyway.
20. **Mirror branch, sourced atmosphere:** clear-sky transmission for reflected sunlight is ~0.64 at zenith and ~0.13 at pass ends (Hottel, via Çelik & McInnes; R1-136), not the handoff's 0.85 → full-capture yield 313.8 → 236.3 W per m² of mirror at zenith (R1-154). Reflect Orbital's 50 W/m² target needs ~3,000 Earendil-1-size mirrors over one spot (~4,700 with atmosphere; R1-124).
21. **Microwave beaming from LEO is blocked by the transmitter, not the solar array.** At the measured 28 W/kg RF-out (JAXA 2015 GaN array, R2-043) the transmitter alone costs 92 kg per continuous kW even with weightless PV and perfect coverage — above orbital GPUs' 13–19 kg/kW. Break-even needs ~194 W/kg RF-out (7× today). Verdict v1: B-LEO loses at current and next tiers; Monte Carlo P(B lighter than A) = 3.3% (E021). *[E023 corrected this to 4.4%; E028 replaced the single "probability" with a prior-variant sensitivity table (0.000–0.049 of draws), and added: at the tier-baseline 88 W/kg array no transmitter value flips current-tier B-LEO.]*
22. **The preregistered mass metric favours mirrors, and mass is not their binding constraint.** Mirrors beat orbital GPUs on launched mass at every tier (even Reflect's filed Earendil-1 system: 9 kg/kW over 15 y vs 40–57) *[WRONG, corrected E028 (#29): at Earendil-1's filed ~1-y life it is 45.6 kg/kW in clear sky — not lighter; only sail-class density wins]*, but only work as mega-constellations: clearing the 2.6 W/m² inverter start needs ≥0.12 km² of mirror over each spot at once (~384 Earendil-1s per site, ~8×10⁴ for ~4 GW). Their real constraints — weather, night-sky impact, low-light PV, constellation scale — are outside the mass metric and must be quantified before any "mirrors win" claim (E021).
23. **My own Monte Carlo was biased against microwave beaming (fixed, E023):** transmitter DC-RF efficiency was held at 0.70 even in draws using the measured 0.14 chain, overstating transmitter mass. Tying it to the chain draw moves P(B lighter than orbital GPUs) from 3.3% to 4.4% — conclusion unchanged, now without the bias.
24. **Light does not fix the memory wall (H5 killed for on-package memory).** 74% of GH200's measured 11.68 pJ/bit HBM access is control-plane logic; storage and sensing are ~1%, and the interposer I/O an optical link could replace is only 2–7%. Commercial optical links cost 2.3–5 pJ/bit, so attaching HBM optically adds 13–37% to fetch energy (R9-001/016/024). Optics' data-movement win is real only at rack-to-cluster reach (co-packaged ~4–6 vs 13–21 pJ/bit pluggable/SerDes, R9-028/030). Also: GPU weight streaming is 18.0 pJ/bit end to end (HBM + L2 + L1), ~54% above the 11.68 used in E014 — photonic comparisons unchanged since both pay it (E024). *[Corrected E028: a photonic engine pays HBM + its own SRAM, not the GPU's L2/L1 path (R9-005 note); decode gain cap becomes ≤ 1.9× — still the memory wall.]*
25. **My H1 as first composed was physically inconsistent (caught by R8, corrected E025).** Time integration sums a dot product of length k·T, so the weights must change every cycle; it cannot be combined with weights held fixed in non-volatile cells. E015's levers L2+ and E018's H1 also omitted the energy of encoding streamed weights. Corrected: streamed-weight designs pay DAC + memory per weight (decode stays at parity, 1.05–1.21×; prefill 2.1–5.1×); resident-weight designs (H1') accumulate spatially with a wide crossbar instead (core 1.9–2.4 fJ/MAC, 80–302× below B300) but only for small models. *[Numbers superseded by E028 (#31): the wide k > n crossbar violates power conservation and the FP8 end mixed precisions.]*
26. **Non-volatile photonic weights cannot hold an LLM.** Measured/upper-bound densities 5.8–3,183 weights/mm² vs 4.76 M/mm² for N2 SRAM (R8-084/104): Qwen2.5-72B would need 23–12,500 m² (R8-124). Time-sharing a crossbar wears out PCM/memresonator/ferroelectric cells within one model pass (R8 dossier).
27. **Optical pre-detection saves on-board energy, not downlink (H2 narrowed).** With 67% cloudy scenes, optical triage before detection and electronic triage after it send the same 43.8 vs 132.7 Mbit per 100 tiles (R10-084); optics' value is cutting on-board triage energy ~4–9× and relieving the compute deadline — contingent on a flight-grade passive encoder that no one has demonstrated. Scene light cannot feed single-mode photonic chips (0.17 pW per mode per THz at 550 nm, 1.39 pW at 1,550 nm, R10-079, recomputed via D18); only free-space multimode optics with non-negative weights apply.
28. **My GEO microwave "win" was a modelling error (Codex + Opus reviews, E028).** v1.1 held the GEO transmitter at a fixed 1 km aperture for any power; a JAXA-class panel (44.7 kg/m², 28 W/kg RF-out, R2-043) of that size radiates at most 0.98 GW RF, while 10 GW delivered needs 17–22 GW RF. Sized by the power it radiates (D20), B-GEO needs a 36–69-year life to match orbital GPUs vs a 15-year design life — it loses at current and next tiers; break-even transmitter ≥ 130 W/kg (4.7×) for next-tier GEO.
29. **Reflect's filed mirror is not lighter than orbital GPUs at its own filed life (Opus review, E028).** Earendil-1's FCC filing states ~1 year of operations and a ~5-month drag deorbit (R1-115/116); v1 gave it a 5-year life borrowed from data-centre filings. At 1 y it is 45.6 kg/kW over 15 y in clear sky (A: 40–57) and loses with any weather (break-even clear sky > 1.0). Sail-class density (flown ACS3 spacecraft, 200 g/m², R1-151 — v1 used membrane + booms only, 12.5 g/m², mislabelled "sail system") wins only with ideal coverage and ≥ 52% clear sky.
30. **Double-counted HBM in my GPU decode reference (Codex review, E028).** B300's 187/560 fJ/MAC (R3-070) is TGP ÷ peak throughput, and TGP already includes HBM; adding 18 pJ/bit of fetch on top counted memory twice. Replaced by the TGP roofline (1,400 W ÷ (8 TB/s × batch)); the decode conclusion survives: any weight-streaming engine is capped at ≤ 1.9× a B300.
31. **H1' as stated in A1 broke power conservation and mixed precisions (Opus + Codex reviews, E028).** A passive single-wavelength network with k = 4096 inputs and n = 128 outputs delivers ≤ n/k of its light to the detectors, so the laser cannot amortise over k (D13 correction 3); and "80–302× below B300" paired a 5-bit read-out with FP8. Restated with square layers and matched pairs: core 0.91–21.7 fJ/MAC, support overhead allowed for 10× is 2.6–20.6× (vs 318× measured); with a conventional receiver H1' fails at every size.
32. **H1' is not a new architecture (R11 prior-art search).** MIT US11546077B2 (priority 2021) already claims static zero-power weights, charge summation and receiverless photodetector→modulator links; US10268232B2 (2016) and UC US11144821B2 (2017) cover other triples. The contribution left is the energy budget and the measurement no one has published: full-system wall-plug support overhead of a resident-weight, receiverless engine.
33. **Resident-weight photonic inference does not beat a B300 by 10× when built from measured parts (E031 review, E032 ladder; Pavle chose to publish it as a negative result).** The idealised H1' leaves 10–21× for support overhead, but only by measuring precision against the largest possible 1,024-term sum (typical outputs ≈ 0.17 LSB) and by assuming an unsourced 1 fF node and 20 dB loss. With the measured-parts node (R8-128), the O-E-O device's own operating point (R7-046) and precision over real outputs (D21), the budget is 2.65× at NVFP4 pairing and below 1× at FP8; 6 dB more loss erases it. The only measured full photonic processor spends up to 318× (R3-045).
34. **Break-even lives computed with fractional spacecraft (Codex + Opus draft reviews, E033).** Counting whole launches, the filed mirror needs ≥ 1.88 y (not 1.7), and no service life lets current- or next-tier GEO beaming match orbital GPUs within the lifecycle: one launch already exceeds their total.
35. **A dawn-dusk orbit does not deliver continuous power to any one site (Opus draft review, D22).** An equatorial receiver sees a terminator-following satellite ~8.8% of the day; the network-level "continuous" delivery of B-LEO and C needs storage or a grid linking sites, which the model does not count (favourable to B and C).
36. **Our ledger had a ×10 unit slip (R5-031): land for a 90%-solar off-grid data centre is ~102 km² per GW of continuous load, not 10.2.** Found by the fresh-context paper review; corrected in the research file and re-merged (E033).
37. **Mirror light was converted at 30% on every lit square metre (Codex final review, E034).** A real tracking solar farm holds 59.3 MW_DC per km² at full sun (R5-077), about 5.9% of the light landing on its site. Aimed at a real farm, sail-density mirrors are ~105 kg/kW over 15 y (not 20.8) and the filed Earendil-1 ~231 (not 45.6); only a bare membrane stays lighter than orbital GPUs. Mirrors need a dedicated, fully covered receiver to win, whose area and cost the model does not include.
